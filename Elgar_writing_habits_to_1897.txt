ELGAR'S PRIVATE WRITING HABITS, 1879 - 14 JULY 1897
Derived from his own letters, postcards and sketches in J.N. Moore, Letters of a Lifetime, pp. 1-59
(about 13,300 words of private writing before the cipher). Counts and short phrases only:

1. "&" INSTEAD OF "AND"
   Private letters: "&" 429 times, "and" written out 50 times  ->  about 9 in 10 "and"s are "&".
   His one newspaper article (1886): "&" 0 times, "and" 36 times. So "&" is his PRIVATE habit.
   Elgar's cipher alphabet has 24 symbols and none for "&". If he wrote the Dorabella note the way he
   wrote his letters, its plain text may hold very few "AND"s - unless "&" was written some other way
   (the cipher's single dot? an omitted word?). Our English scoring model expects "AND" to be one of
   the commonest words, which slightly penalises a decrypt that leaves it out.

2. ABBREVIATIONS HE USES REPEATEDLY
   wd. (would, 11x)   Xmas (11x)   yrs. / Yrs.   wh (which)   cd. (could)   shd.   recd.   accts.
   tho' / thro' / altho'   'tis  'twill  'twould   'em   'till   o'c (o'clock)   &c.
   Months and days:  Jany.  Augt.  Septr  Novr.  Decr.  Wedy  Saty
   Places:  Birm.  Worcr.  Wrstr.     Signatures:  Ted.  Ed: Elgar  Edwd.  E.E.  E.W.E.  E.

3. PLAY WITH WORDS AND LETTERS (relevant to a puzzle written for Dora)
   To Dora Penny, 4 March 1897 - the only letter to her before the cipher:
     "warbling wigorously in Worcester wunce a week (alliteration archaically Norse)"
     - deliberate misspelling for a sound joke (v -> w, o -> wu), and the same letter shows him
     "die-sinking": cutting his own wax seal with an "E" so his works "may be E-sily distinguished".
   Elsewhere: "the Scotish (with one t) lay", "Cantanthem (new word)", "Sintax!", "pro-ey",
   "japed together", "B.O.F.", "God wot!", "Selah!", "fax" (facts), "acquent" (acquainted).

4. FAVOURITE QUOTATIONS HE REPEATS
   Much Ado About Nothing, used twice (1889, 1892), slightly reworded each time:
     "call the rest of the watch together & thank the Lord you are rid of a knave"
   Pepys, twice: "& so home, as Pepys hath it" / "as old Pepys says".
   Burns: "the best laid schemes of mice & men aft gang agley".

5. FORMULAS (open and close of almost every letter)
   "My dear doctor", "glad to get your cheery letter", "please give my kind regards to Mrs.Buck",
   "Believe me / Sincerely yours", "Yours ever", "With much love".
